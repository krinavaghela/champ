02 / Overview
The idea

What if Feynman could sit next to every child, every evening?

Great teachers who are deeply passionate, endlessly patient and fluent in every language are scarce. Generative AI makes a new kind of school possible: experts design the stories and the answer keys, and an AI teaching assistant guides each learner through them, one question at a time.

Problem

Video lessons teach to the average

Kids watch, nod, and forget. Nobody notices when they're stuck, and nobody waits for them.

Solution

A patient teacher, live, out loud

A cartoon scientist tells the story, listens for as long as you need, and gives hints instead of answers.

Role

Product design

Research, UX strategy, information architecture, character & brand, UI, motion and a working prototype.

Built with

React · Claude · ElevenLabs

Claude powers the teachers' thinking; ElevenLabs gives each one a distinct voice and accent.

03 / Research
Competitive research

Great content exists. A patient teacher with a story doesn't.

3Blue1Brown

Beautiful, but you only watch

The gradient-descent lesson rolls a ball down a surface, then a network with 13,002 weights. Animations and one quiz question. No interactive version, no AI, no story.

Brilliant + Koji

Closest competitor

Problem-first puzzles with instant feedback. Koji, an animated AI tutor launched in May 2026, waves, nods and speaks, but has no mouth and no story world. It reacts; it doesn't guide a narrative.

Eureka Labs

The right model, not shipped

"Teacher + AI symbiosis": a human designs the course, an AI assistant guides each student. Its first course, LLM101n, is still unreleased.

Opportunity

A story world where the story is the concept, taught live by a character who speaks at fixed moments, asks "why did you choose that way?", and only gives the next hint when the learner is still stuck.

04 / Users
Who we're designing for

Two people open Champ: the learner, and the grown-up who trusts it.

Proto-persona · Learner

Maya, 11, "the question machine"

Loves robots, drawing and stories. Asks "but why?" until adults run out of answers. Gets bored by long videos and anxious when she's wrong in front of others.

  • Needs: someone who never runs out of patience
  • Needs: to try first, then be helped
  • Motivated by: building something real, collecting, streaks
Proto-persona · Parent

Priya, 41, "is this screen time good screen time?"

Wants her daughter ready for an AI world, but worries about screen time, privacy and AI that just hands out answers.

  • Needs: to see what was learned, not just minutes
  • Needs: limits and controls behind a grown-ups-only gate
  • Trusts: transparency, no ads, data on the device

05 / Principles
Design principles

Five rules every screen had to pass.

Story first

The story is the concept

Gradient descent is being lost in fog on a mountain. Tokenization is Tagore chopping words like vegetables.

Ask, don't tell

The hint ladder

Guiding question → small hint → bigger hint → full explanation, only if still stuck.

Patient by design

Waits for a real pause

Voice input waits 2.4 s of silence before answering, so the teacher never cuts a child off.

Learner in control

Stop, mute, undo

One tap or Esc stops any teacher. Mute keeps captions. Every change can be undone.

Habits over scores

Animal mindsets

Badges reward courage, focus and curiosity, not just correct answers.

UX laws applied

Fitts · Hick · Jakob · Doherty threshold · Goal-gradient · Zeigarnik · Peak-end · Von Restorff · Aesthetic-usability · Nielsen's 10 heuristics · WCAG focus, captions & reduced motion

10 / Characters
The teachers

42 cartoon teachers, one parametric drawing system.

Every character is drawn in code from a short description: face shape, age, hair, nose, facial hair, glasses and outfit. They breathe, blink, wave, nod while you talk and lip-sync to their own ElevenLabs voice. They are cartoon AI characters inspired by real people, not the real people.
